01 / Research Object

Anonymous communication under dynamic conditions.

Shye is an ongoing study of anonymous communication protocols. It considers networks whose relationships do not remain fixed and whose conditions keep changing, asking how a strictly centreless communication architecture can be defined, evaluated and implemented.

This is a public research brief, not a paper abstract or a statement of security or usability. It answers only three questions: what is being studied, where the work intends to go, and what it cannot claim today.

02 / Public Concepts

Three concepts, one research direction.

These terms describe the problem space the research intends to face. They are not proof that the current work has already achieved its aims.

01 / SHORT-LIVED

Short-lived

Communication relationships and observable structures do not remain fixed. The research asks what this change enables and costs.

S
02 / CENTRELESS

Centreless

Strict centrelessness is a design goal. We do not claim that it has already been achieved or validated.

C
03 / CHAOTIC

Chaotic

This means real environments with continuously changing, hard-to-predict participants and network conditions—not mathematical chaos theory.

H

03 / Intended Destination

The destination is verifiability, not a slogan.

The intended state of Shye is a “verifiable research protocol”.

To become a verifiable research protocol, supported by a rigorous paper, a reference prototype and reproducible experiments that others can question, test and extend.

Theory, engineering and experiment must correspond to one another. If one part is incomplete, the other two cannot stand in as evidence for it.

04 / Disclosure Boundary

The responsible move now is to keep the boundary.

The research is not public yet. This page therefore withholds detail and refuses to present an intended capability as an established result.

  • Shye is not a production protocol or a deployable service.
  • It promises neither absolute anonymity nor guaranteed delivery nor already-proven security.
  • The manuscript is not public; no title, abstract, PDF or version roadmap is provided.
  • Protocol mechanisms, parameters, a specific threat model, experiment figures and results are not currently disclosed.

Material will enter this site only after it is accessible from a formal public source and receives explicit release approval. The site build never reads internal research files.

05 / Status & Discussion

The research continues; the conclusions remain open.

At launch, discussion, criticism and review can address the research object, intended destination and boundaries published here. Questions requiring unreleased material will not be answered yet.
